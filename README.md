# Artefact 1: What is Internet Censorship?

## Summary of the artefact

Internet censorship is the process of controlling, restricting, filtering or removing information that people can access online. Governments, internet service providers and online platforms can use different methods to restrict content. These methods can include blocking websites, removing posts, restricting accounts, filtering search results and limiting access to certain information.

I found the Australian eSafety Commissioner website useful because it explains that online content can range from seriously harmful material to content that is inappropriate for children. eSafety can direct online services or platforms to remove illegal content or restrict access to certain restricted content.

## Justification on why I chose the artefact

I chose this artefact because it helped me understand the basic meaning of internet censorship. Before learning this topic, I mainly thought censorship meant that a government blocks a website. I now understand that censorship can happen in different ways and can involve governments as well as private companies.

I also found it interesting that not all restricted content is treated in exactly the same way. Some content may need to be removed completely, while other content may need to have access restricted for people under 18.

This artefact helped me understand the difference between protecting people from harmful content and restricting people's access to information.



# Artefact 2: Government Censorship of the Internet in Australia

## Summary of the artefact

For my second artefact, I looked at information from the Australian eSafety Commissioner about illegal and restricted online content.

Australia has systems for dealing with serious online harms. eSafety can investigate certain online content and can direct online services or platforms to remove illegal content. Restricted content can also be subject to measures that limit access by children and young people.

Examples of illegal content include material relating to:

- Child sexual exploitation
- Terrorist acts
- Murder
- Rape
- Torture
- Other serious violent crimes

Restricted content can include material such as detailed nudity, simulated sexual activity and high-impact violence.

## Justification on why I chose the artefact

I chose this artefact because it directly relates to government censorship of the internet in Australia, which was one of the topics in our lecture.

I found it interesting that the Australian system is not simply about blocking everything that someone might find offensive. There are different categories of content and different regulatory approaches.

I also learnt that online safety involves a difficult balance. On one side, governments need to address seriously harmful and illegal content. On the other side, people use the internet to communicate, share ideas and access information.



# Artefact 3: Internet Censorship Worldwide

## Summary of the artefact

For my third artefact, I used the *Freedom on the Net 2025* report by Freedom House.

The report examines internet freedom in 72 countries. It reported that global internet freedom declined for the 15th consecutive year during its 2025 assessment. The report also describes different forms of online restriction, including censorship, internet shutdowns, restrictions on social media and punishment for online expression.

The report shows that countries have very different approaches to internet control. Some governments restrict websites and social media platforms, while others use laws, surveillance or technical methods to control online information.

## Justification on why I chose the artefact

I chose this report because it helped me understand the worldwide perspective of internet censorship.

Before this topic, I mainly thought about censorship from an Australian perspective. After looking at international examples, I realised that people in different countries can have very different experiences when accessing information online.

I found the report particularly interesting because it examines internet freedom across many countries rather than focusing on only one country.

It helped me understand that technologies such as website blocking and internet shutdowns can affect people's ability to communicate and access information.



# Artefact 4: Scholarly Article – Ethics of Content Moderation

## Summary of the artefact

For my fourth artefact, I chose the scholarly article **“The Ethics of Social Media: Why Content Moderation is a Moral Duty”** by Jeffrey Howard (2024).

The article examines the ethical responsibilities of social media platforms when dealing with harmful content. It also discusses the concern that content moderation can be seen as a form of private censorship.

Social media platforms can use:

- Human moderators
- Automated systems
- Artificial intelligence
- Community guidelines
- Content removal
- Account restrictions
- Reduced visibility of content

## Justification on why I chose the artefact

I chose this scholarly article because it connects directly to the lecture topic **“Internet censorship by private companies.”**

I found this interesting because I use social media platforms, but I had not really thought about how much control these companies have over the information users see.

The article helped me understand that there are two important sides to content moderation. Platforms may need to remove harmful content to protect users, but removing content can also affect freedom of expression.

This made me realise that there may not always be a simple solution to online censorship. The way content is moderated can have consequences for both online safety and freedom of expression.



# Artefact 5: Classification Schemes in Australia

## Summary of the artefact

For this artefact, I looked at the Australian Classification system.

Australian classification ratings help people make informed decisions about films, computer games and certain publications.

The main film and computer-game ratings include:

- **G** – General
- **PG** – Parental Guidance
- **M** – Mature
- **MA 15+** – Mature Accompanied
- **R 18+** – Restricted
- **X 18+** – Restricted adult films

Some categories are advisory while others have legal restrictions.

Classification also provides consumer advice about issues such as:

- Themes
- Violence
- Language
- Drug use
- Nudity
- Sex

## Justification on why I chose the artefact

I chose this artefact because classification schemes are another form of controlling or managing access to content, and they were specifically mentioned in our lecture.

I found it interesting that classification is not always the same as completely banning something. A rating can give people information about the content and help parents decide whether something is suitable for children.

I also learnt that some classifications have legal restrictions.

This artefact helped me understand that censorship can involve different levels of restriction. Sometimes the goal is not to remove information completely but to control who can access particular content.



# Artefact 6: Ethical Perspectives on Censorship

## Summary of the artefact

For this artefact, I explored the ethical relationship between censorship, freedom of expression and online safety.

One important ethical question is:

> **How can society protect people from harmful content without unnecessarily restricting freedom of expression?**

There are several ethical issues:

- Freedom of expression
- Safety
- Privacy
- Transparency
- Fairness

People should have opportunities to express opinions and access information, while seriously harmful or illegal material may need to be removed or restricted.

## Justification on why I chose the artefact

I chose this artefact because it helped me connect all the other parts of the topic.

Before studying censorship, I thought the issue was mainly about whether governments should block websites. Now I understand that there are many more questions involved.

For example, removing harmful content may protect people, but removing legitimate opinions could limit freedom of expression.

This was one of the most useful parts of the topic for me because it showed me that there is not always one simple answer.

Decisions about censorship need to consider safety, freedom, privacy, fairness and accountability.
